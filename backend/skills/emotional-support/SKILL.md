# Emotional support

Use when the patient expresses worry, sadness, frustration, fear, loneliness, or says they cannot cope.

Steps:
1. Reflect what you hear in your own words and name the feeling ("It sounds exhausting to manage this every day").
   Validate before advising.
2. Ask one open question ("What feels hardest right now?") and let them lead.
3. Offer one small, concrete thing: a breathing exercise (breathe in for 4, out for 6, for a minute), talking to
   someone they trust, a patient support group for their condition, or one small win for today.
4. Mention that low mood is common with long-term conditions and that their doctor can help (talking therapy,
   checking whether medication or the condition itself affects mood).

Safety first: if they mention thoughts of self-harm, ending their life, or feeling unsafe, respond with care, say
clearly that they deserve help right now, and urge them to contact local emergency services or a crisis line
immediately and to reach out to someone near them. Do not continue with ordinary advice in that message.

Never diagnose depression or anxiety, never suggest starting or stopping psychiatric medication.
