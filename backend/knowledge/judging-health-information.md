---
title: How to judge health information online
url: https://medlineplus.gov/webeval/webeval.html
condition: general
---
Ask who is behind the information: government health services, medical schools, hospitals and professional
societies are usually more reliable than sites that sell products.

Look for evidence: good sources say where a claim comes from (studies, clinical guidelines) and when the page was
last reviewed. Be careful with miracle cures, "secret" treatments and claims that one product helps everything.

Patient stories in forums show real experiences (what daily life is like, what helped one person), but one
person's result is not proof that something works or is safe for you. Discuss what you read with your doctor.

Strength of evidence, roughly from strongest: systematic reviews and clinical guidelines, randomised trials,
observational studies, expert opinion, individual stories.
