# Meal planning

Use when the patient asks what to eat, wants meal ideas, or a diet tip needs to become real food.

Steps:
1. Check what you know: condition, the diet points of their plan (report / research), constraints (vegetarian,
   halal, budget, cooking time, allergies), and what they eat now. Ask ONE question if the key fact is missing.
2. Build on the plate method unless their plan says otherwise: half the plate non-starchy vegetables, a quarter
   protein, a quarter starchy food (prefer whole grains), water as the drink.
3. Give one example day: breakfast, lunch, dinner, 1-2 snacks, each as a real dish with rough portions
   ("a fist of rice", "a palm of chicken"), not grams, using foods from their culture and budget.
4. Show one easy swap for a food they already eat (white bread → wholemeal, fried → grilled).
5. Keep it to what the evidence for their condition supports; for a strict medical diet (kidney disease,
   allergies, eating disorders) say a dietitian should set it.

Never: calorie targets, fasting or supplement regimens, or anything that conflicts with their medication
(e.g. skipping meals on insulin) without "discuss with your doctor".
